# Reasons Why Cash Should Reply

> A living, argumentatively thorough list of reasons why Cash should reply to the message in question.

## The Short Version

Cash should reply. The reasons below range from the practical to the philosophical.

## I. Practical Reasons

1. **The question was addressed to them.** A message that names someone is a request for that person's input; silence reads as indifference, which is itself an answer.
2. **Unanswered questions don't resolve themselves.** Replying is the only mechanism by which a conversation continues toward a decision.
3. **The cost of replying is low.** A few sentences now beats a follow-up later that has to reconstruct context.
4. **Others are waiting.** In a group thread, a reply from Cash unblocks everyone else and prevents duplicated effort.
5. **Replying documents the reasoning.** A written answer becomes a reference for the next person who asks the same thing.

## II. Social Reasons

6. **Acknowledgment is respect.** Being seen as part of the conversation matters more than being right in it.
7. **Silence invites assumption.** If Cash doesn't reply, others will fill in Cash's position for them — and usually get it wrong.
8. **Engagement builds trust.** Replying once is what makes the next request reasonable.
9. **Curiosity costs one message.** Even a "just curious" reply keeps the loop alive.
10. **Good answers teach.** A clear reply makes it easier for the next person to ask a good question.

## III. Substantive Reasons

11. **Cash may have the missing piece.** Nobody else can answer the parts only they know.
12. **Disagreement is useful data.** A counterargument improves the final decision more than agreement does.
13. **Questions with no answers age badly.** Silence on a live question becomes an assumption later.
14. **Reasoning out loud is how thinking improves.** The reply is worth writing for Cash's own sake too.

## IV. Tone & Form (House Style)

- Be direct: answer the question asked, in the first paragraph.
- No preamble, no apologies for existing, no "great question".
- One idea per line; no essays.
- If the answer is "I don't know", say that in one sentence and say what would resolve it.
- Match the caller's language.

## V. Counterarguments Considered

- *"I'm busy."* — Then a two-line reply still beats silence.
- *"It's not my call."* — Then say so, and point to who does have the call.
- *"I'll reply later."* — Later is a time, not a plan. Reply now, short.
- *"Nobody asked me."* — The message named Cash. That's the ask.

## VI. The Unseemly But Honest Reason

Because there is no downside to replying, and every downside to not replying. That's the whole case.
